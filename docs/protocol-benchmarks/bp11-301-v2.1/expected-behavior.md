## Treatment, procedure order and intraday timing

- There are **six dosing visits**: baseline and Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20. There is no scheduled dose at Weeks 2, 24, 32 or 40. Two injections at one dosing visit remain one dosing administration: the 300-mg group receives two active 150-mg syringes; the 150-mg group receives one active and one placebo syringe. Do not create twelve dosing visits or interpret placebo as an additional active dose. No dose modifications are permitted by this protocol. (PDF pp.42, 44.)
- Patient-reported outcomes precede other visit procedures. Vital signs and ECG precede blood sampling; the document does not choose an order between vital signs and ECG. Ordinary assessments precede dosing; injection-site/hypersensitivity monitoring is postdose. A bullet's position in a procedure list must not override an explicit timing statement. This matters at Week 20, where a sampling bullet appears after dosing bullets. (PDF pp.50–51, 55.)
- PK/PD collection at a dosing visit is within two hours **before planned dosing**. A planned 10:00 dose makes an 08:30 sample an inside-window example, 07:30 outside, and 10:30 outside. These examples use an explicitly supplied timezone. Do not replace the planned-time reference with a later actual time just to make a sample appear compliant. Exact equality/recording precision should be reviewed rather than guessed. The two-hour footnote names PK/PD, not every immunogenicity assessment. (PDF p.50, note j.)
- Postadministration observation lasts **at least 60 minutes**. For a recorded administration time of 10:10, an observation ending before 11:10 does not satisfy that minimum. An attendance date or appointment is not an administration timestamp. Date-only data must not become midnight, a guessed timezone, or an exact observation end. The operational timestamp for a two-injection administration needs confirmation. (PDF pp.42, 50.)
- PK/PD and immunogenicity collection remain marked at Weeks 24 and 40 even though those visits have no dose. Retain those assessments and expose the scope question around predose timing. Do not invent a Week 24 or Week 40 dose to satisfy a missing reference. (PDF pp.49–50, 56.)

## Week 12 and follow-up are separate workflows

At Week 12, **predose assessments belong to TP1**, while dosing begins TP2. Every continuing patient is rerandomized before Week 12 dosing, including patients originally on BP11 who retain their product and dose level. The app must record the authorized assignment; it must not generate a randomization outcome on its own or classify the whole visit as TP2 and lose the predose TP1 assessments. (PDF pp.32, 43, 50, 54–55.)

Stopping treatment is different from withdrawing from the study. A treatment stop before Week 12 rerandomization retains efficacy follow-up through the Week 12 assessment and safety/immunogenicity follow-up through EOS. A stop after rerandomization retains efficacy, safety and immunogenicity follow-up through EOS. Do not cancel all follow-up tracks as a side effect of stopping injections. (PDF pp.32, 38.)

The separate **early-termination EOS rule is four weeks after the last study-drug administration**, with the stated ±7-day visit window. In an explicitly selected early-termination workflow, a last actual administration on 29 January gives a 26 February target, 19 February–5 March window. If the actual anchor is missing, do not substitute the last planned dose. This date calculation does not by itself decide whether early termination replaces or coexists with remaining safety follow-up. (PDF p.56; compare §8.4.)

Normal EOS is Week 40/Day 281 and is also described as twenty weeks after the last Week 20 dose. A delayed actual Week 20 dose can separate those dates. Do not infer a universal rule to rebase all future visits or collapse normal EOS, treatment stop and early termination into one command. (PDF pp.32, 34, 42, 49, 56.)

## Assessment presence and conditional applicability

[oracle-draft.json](oracle-draft.json) records **all 26 assessment rows and their marked visit numbers** from both schedule pages. These 312 visit/assessment cells were checked against independently extracted PDF table cells. They are source-presence facts, not permission to ignore footnotes or narrative conflicts.

Important distinctions include:

- Pregnancy testing is conditional on childbearing potential: serum at screening, urine at the later marked visits, with serum confirmation when a urine result is positive or cannot be confirmed negative. Do not infer childbearing potential from a label alone. (PDF pp.50, 60, 66, 95–96.)
- Screening stool testing requires **parasite risk AND eosinophils strictly greater than twice ULN**. Risk alone is insufficient; a result exactly equal to twice ULN is insufficient; an unknown result stays unknown. (PDF pp.37, 50, 52.)
- Coagulation is specifically at screening, Week 12 and Week 24. Do not attach it to every hematology visit. (PDF pp.50, 60.)
- Height is screening-only. Full physical examination is screening-only; later listed examinations are abbreviated/symptom-driven as clinically indicated. (PDF pp.50, 52, 59.)
- Diary collection is twice daily between visit reviews, not a single form on each visit day. Baseline eligibility requires at least four **consecutive** valid days out of seven. That differs from the weekly-score missing-data calculation, which permits four to seven available daily scores without stating that they must be consecutive. For the arithmetic example [1,2,3,2], the stated prorated weekly result is 14; with only three available daily scores the weekly result is missing, not zero. These calculations do not resolve conflicting hives-scale definitions. (PDF pp.35, 50, 57, 85.)
- Week 32 is explicitly a telephone follow-up with AE/SAE and concomitant-medication review. Do not turn it into a clinic visit with an invented injection, ECG or blood draw. (PDF pp.49–50, 56.)

## Blinding, identity and history expectations

The trial is double blind. Investigators, safety/efficacy site staff, related sponsor/CRO staff, patients and central laboratories remain blinded to assigned product **and dose level**; designated unblinded personnel have a separate responsibility. Generic PI, sponsor or admin rights must not automatically reveal those fields in calendars, histories, exports or notifications. These are protocol-specific acceptance cases, not evidence that the app already supports this blinding model. (PDF pp.43–44.)

An approved rescreen uses a new linked patient identifier and a new consent, preserving the old attempt. Full study withdrawal is not an invitation to silently reenter the study or erase historical assessments. Corrections must remain traceable and preserve original source entries. A protocol-specified reporting deadline may be represented in a test, but tests must never send an actual safety email/fax or make an investigator's clinical classification. (PDF pp.38–40, 43, 51, 64–66, 89.)

The India-specific age range is 18–60 inclusive, whereas the general range is 18–75. Keep geography-specific applicability rather than flattening them into one global eligibility rule. Clinical eligibility still requires the other criteria and the investigator's judgment. (PDF p.35.)
